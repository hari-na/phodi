# Phodi — Story Bible

A single-source narrative reference for chapter generation. Update this
*before* writing a chapter, not after. If a chapter contradicts the bible,
either rewrite the chapter or update the bible — never let them drift.

---

## The premise

You moved to Bangalore for your first engineering job. You have thirty days
to find a flat, hold down the job, and figure out whether this city is
yours. The catch: the city speaks Kannada and you don't. (Yet.)

**Scope decision (2026-05-16, updated):** the narrative arc is **100
days** — your character's first hundred days in Bangalore, roughly the
length of a real-world probation period. We build **30 of those days
as playable chapters**; the rest are time passing between key moments.
A "Day N" tag on a chapter is the *narrative* day inside the 100-day
arc, not the chapter index — so chapters 1, 2, 3 happen on Days 1, 2,
3, but chapter 11 might happen on Day 17 or 22 of your arc.

This shape lets the story breathe. You don't grind through 100 chapters
to feel time pass — the gaps between chapters *are* the time.

## You

- An engineer. Mid-twenties.
- Your origin is **never specified**. The game does not say where you're
  from, what your last name is, what your caste is, what your politics are.
  You are *you*.
- At onboarding the game asks one question:
  > **What languages do you already speak?**
  > ☐ Tamil ☐ Malayalam ☐ Hindi ☐ Telugu ☐ Just English
- That answer determines which bridges show up in lessons and dialogue
  hints. **Nothing else** about the story changes based on it.

## What you accumulate

| Score | What it tracks | How it grows |
|---|---|---|
| **Fluency** | Kannada correctness | Correct word-bank builds, right MC answers, hint-free choices |
| **Vibes** | Cultural appropriateness | Right register (ನೀವು vs ನೀನು), politeness, tact, restraint |
| **Flags** | Branching state | "tipped Ravi well," "Amma noticed your mistake," "Anika is amused" |

## Hint progression

Three difficulty bands as the player advances. The toggle to reveal English
meaning is always available — but tapping it zeros out Fluency for that beat.

| Band | Days | Default surface |
|---|---|---|
| **Survival** | 1-10 | Kannada + translit + English meaning + bridge for your L1 |
| **Living** | 11-20 | Kannada + translit + bridge. English hidden behind "Show meaning" — costs Fluency. |
| **Belonging** | 21-30 | Kannada + translit only. Bridge hidden behind toggle — costs Fluency. |
| *Optional Hard Mode* | any | Audio + translit only. No script shown. |

The translit never disappears — it's the phonetic pronunciation guide and
the game's whole accessibility floor. You're learning to *speak* Kannada,
not to read its script.

## The arc

### Act 1 — Survival (Days 1-10)

You land. Everything is loud. Your company's promised flat fell through.
Hotel for a week. Wallet thin. The city hasn't decided whether to be kind.

1. **The Airport Auto.** Ravi the late-shift driver. Negotiate a fair fare
   without sounding like fresh meat. *(SHIPPED.)*
2. **The Lobby.** Hotel check-in at midnight. Formal Kannada, the polite
   receptionist who's seen a hundred you's this month.
3. **The First Chai.** Anna's stall outside Indiranagar Metro. Becomes a
   recurring chorus character — sees everyone come and go.
4. **The Broker Call.** Rangaswamy. Phone-only. No body language to read.
   He's evasive about furnishings, advance, exact rent. You probe.
5. **The First Flat.** A 2BHK in HSR. Too expensive, half-furnished, the
   owner is "out of town" (suspicious). You walk.
6. **The Second Flat.** Indiranagar. Smaller. Owner is there — an uncle
   from Mysuru. Cultural test: can you talk to him without sounding like
   a kid trying too hard?
7. **The Deposit.** Owner wants ten months' advance. Broker wants his cut.
   You push back, in Kannada, in front of both. Vibes matters here.
8. **Moving In.** Day-one meet with the watchman, Lokesh. He'll be in
   every chapter from here. Set the tone.
9. **The Neighbor.** Knock on the door. They're curious. They've heard the
   accent. They want to know your story.
10. **First Sunday — the Vegetable Market.** Vendor aunty and her bored
    granddaughter at the cash counter. Bargain without offending. Become
    a regular.

### Act 2 — Living (Days 11-20)

The flat is yours. Now work, food, friends — and someone.

11. **Day One at the Office.** HR is Latha. The "casual" onboarding chat
    is in Kannada. Cultural test disguised as paperwork.
12. **The Canteen.** Lunch with Karthik, the colleague who will become
    your first friend. Office gossip. Decoding which managers to avoid.
13. **The Cook Interview.** You're hiring someone to cook three nights a
    week. Wages, hours, what you'll eat, what's appropriate to ask. Tricky.
14. **The Gas Cylinder.** Runs out mid-dosa-batter. Phone call to the
    supplier, who is grumpy and short-tempered tonight.
15. **The Pharmacy.** You're slightly sick. Describing symptoms in
    Kannada — "ತಲೆ ನೋವು" works, "throat scratch" requires charades.
16. **The Meet.** A bookstore in Church Street. You ask the staff for
    something in your best Kannada. The person next to you is amused and
    corrects your pronunciation. Their name is **Anika**.
17. **Cubbon Park.** First proper meeting with Anika. A walk. Small talk
    that's actually getting-to-know-you. She speaks fast Kannada when
    excited.
18. **Lokesh Knows.** The watchman saw who dropped you off last night.
    Gentle teasing. Vibes test — how do you respond to gossip?
19. **The Festival Invite.** Karthik invites you to his family's Karaga
    celebration. You're now Going To Things.
20. **The Cook's Favor.** She needs an advance for a family emergency.
    Trust, money, register — Vibes-heavy beat.

### Act 3 — Belonging (Days 21-30)

The stakes rise. The shortcuts catch up.

21. **The Invite.** Anika texts: "Amma wants to meet you. Sunday lunch."
    You're going home with her.
22. **The Family Lunch.** Amma is formal, observant. Appa is silent,
    watching. The food is mostly silent. Every word counts.
23. **The Misstep.** You use ನೀನು where ನೀವು was required, addressing
    Amma. Recoverable, but the flag is set. Amma doesn't say a word.
24. **The Work Crisis.** Boss needs you to lead a vendor meeting. Vendors
    speak only Kannada. Your career is on the line. *Fluency matters
    here in a real way.*
25. **The Temple Auntie.** Anika asked you to stand in line for something
    at a temple. Auntie behind you strikes up a conversation that becomes
    a marriage proposal for her niece. Navigate without offending anyone.
26. **The Fight.** You and Anika argue, half-Kannada half-English. About
    whether you're really trying, or performing. The argument exposes the
    Vibes gaps.
27. **The Apology.** You go back to her family home. Amma is in earshot.
    You have to apologise — properly, formally, in Kannada.
28. **The Festival.** A family festival (Ugadi, or a milestone birthday).
    You give a small speech. Six lines. They're all going to remember
    these six lines.
29. **The Eve.** Anika asks the question: will you stay? Your old company
    wants you back home. You haven't told her about the offer.
30. **The Morning.** Three endings, gated by Fluency + Vibes + flags:
    - **You belong here now.** Anika smiles. Lokesh nods. Anna remembers
      your usual order without asking.
    - **You'll figure it out.** You stayed, but the city is still
      learning your name and you're still learning its.
    - **You went home.** Bangalore wasn't yours yet. Maybe it will be
      next time. Anika's last voicemail plays. You carry some of it.

## Ending tree

Endings are computed at Day 30 from cumulative `Fluency`, `Vibes`, and a
small set of pivotal flags.

```
                       Fluency + Vibes
                              ≥ 40                    20-39             < 20
                              ──────                  ─────             ─────
                              You belong              You'll figure     You went home
                              here now                it out
flag: amma_recovered          → "Welcome home."       → "Come back."    → —
flag: amma_unrecovered        → "Try harder."         → "Try harder."   → "Bangalore
                                                                          wasn't yours."
flag: anika_proposed_back     → marriage ending       → "soon" ending   → letter ending
flag: anika_silent_at_end     → bittersweet           → reflective      → cold
```

Four base endings, ~8 variants. The variants are mostly final-line changes
in the same scene — not different scenes. Cheap to generate.

## Voice & writing rules

Same editorial bible as the lessons, with these chapter-specific additions:

- **Stage directions are sparing.** One italic line per beat, max. Show
  what the player needs to know that the audio can't carry (a glance, a
  rolled-up sleeve, the auntie noticing your shoes).
- **The narrator is silent unless absolutely necessary.** Most beats are
  pure dialogue + stage direction.
- **English in NPC speech is allowed when it's realistic** — Karthik
  code-switches, Anika does in heated moments, the broker does when
  cornered. Amma never does.
- **No exposition dumps.** The player learns the rules by getting them
  wrong. Vibes hits are the teaching.

## Resolved questions

- **Anika's gender** — player choice at onboarding ("Who do you want to
  fall for?"). Same dialogue tree, gendered pronouns rendered from the
  selection.
- **Length** — 100-day arc, 30 playable chapters. Chapter cadence is
  variable: dense at the start (Days 1-10 = chapters 1-10), then
  spread out across the next 90 narrative days.

## Open questions

- **Multiplayer / leaderboard.** Defer. Out of scope.
